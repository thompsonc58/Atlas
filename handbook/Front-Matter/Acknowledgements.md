# Acknowledgements

The development of the Atlas Curriculum Engineering Framework has been shaped by the collective knowledge, experience, and dedication of educators, engineers, researchers, and practitioners who continually strive to improve teaching and learning.

Atlas builds upon decades of work in education, instructional design, systems engineering, software engineering, quality management, project management, and organizational learning. While Curriculum Engineering is presented as an emerging discipline, it is founded upon the contributions of many established fields that have demonstrated the value of systematic design, continuous improvement, and evidence-based practice.

The authors gratefully acknowledge the educators who devote themselves to preparing learners for success, the curriculum developers who transform ideas into meaningful learning experiences, and the instructional designers who continually advance the science and practice of education.

Recognition is also extended to the engineering community. Disciplines such as systems engineering, software engineering, industrial engineering, and quality engineering have demonstrated that complex systems benefit from clearly defined processes, standards, governance, documentation, traceability, and continuous improvement. These principles have provided significant inspiration for the Atlas Framework.

The Atlas Framework also benefits from the contributions of accreditation organizations, academic institutions, industry partners, and subject matter experts whose work has advanced educational quality, accountability, and workforce preparation. Their efforts have reinforced the importance of designing educational systems that are measurable, adaptable, and aligned with the needs of learners and society.

Finally, appreciation is extended to the growing Atlas community. Every educator, researcher, developer, reviewer, and practitioner who contributes ideas, feedback, examples, and improvements plays an important role in the continued evolution of the framework. Atlas is intended to be a living body of knowledge that grows through collaboration and shared experience.

Curriculum Engineering is not the work of a single individual or organization. It is a collaborative endeavor dedicated to improving educational systems through the thoughtful application of engineering principles.

To everyone who shares this vision of engineering better learning—thank you.
