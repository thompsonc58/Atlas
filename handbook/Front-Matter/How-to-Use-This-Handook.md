# How to Use This Handbook

## Introduction

The **Atlas Handbook** serves as the foundational body of knowledge for the Atlas Curriculum Engineering Framework (ACEF). It introduces the principles, concepts, methodologies, engineering models, and practices that define the emerging discipline of **Curriculum Engineering**.

Unlike a traditional textbook, this handbook is intended to function as both a learning resource and a professional reference. Readers may study it sequentially to develop a comprehensive understanding of the framework or consult individual chapters as needed throughout the curriculum engineering process.

This document explains how the handbook is organized and provides guidance for navigating the broader Atlas Framework.

---

# Purpose of the Handbook

The primary purpose of the Atlas Handbook is to establish a common body of knowledge for Curriculum Engineering.

The handbook:

- Defines the philosophy of the Atlas Framework.
- Introduces the principles of Curriculum Engineering.
- Describes the Atlas Method.
- Explains the engineering models that support the framework.
- Demonstrates how engineering principles apply to educational systems.
- Provides the conceptual foundation for Atlas Standards, Specifications, Templates, Reference Implementations, and Atlas Studio.

The handbook focuses on understanding the discipline rather than prescribing implementation details.

---

# Intended Audience

The handbook is designed for a diverse audience involved in the design, delivery, governance, and continuous improvement of educational systems.

Examples include:

- Faculty
- Curriculum Developers
- Instructional Designers
- Academic Program Coordinators
- Department Chairs
- Academic Administrators
- Accreditation Teams
- Industry Partners
- Subject Matter Experts
- Educational Researchers
- Software Developers contributing to Atlas Studio

No prior knowledge of the Atlas Framework is required.

# Using the Handbook as a Reference

After completing the handbook once, readers may use it as a reference throughout the curriculum engineering process.

For example:

- Faculty may consult chapters on course and lesson engineering.
- Curriculum developers may reference the Atlas Method while designing new programs.
- Academic leaders may review governance and quality assurance models.
- Researchers may reference the conceptual foundations of Curriculum Engineering.
- Software developers may use the engineering models to support the implementation of Atlas Studio.

---

# Relationship to Atlas Standards

The handbook is **informative**.

Atlas Standards are **normative**.

The handbook explains concepts, principles, methodologies, and engineering models.

Atlas Standards define specific requirements for implementing those concepts consistently.

Readers should consult the appropriate Atlas Standard when developing official curriculum artifacts.

---

# Relationship to Atlas Studio

Atlas Studio is the software implementation of the Atlas Curriculum Engineering Framework.

The handbook explains the concepts.

Atlas Studio provides software tools that assist practitioners in applying those concepts throughout the curriculum engineering lifecycle.

The handbook can be studied independently of Atlas Studio.

The Atlas Handbook introduces more than a framework—it introduces a way of thinking about educational systems through the discipline of engineering.
